## 90. Classification exercises: 40 statements

Use **FR** functional; **NFR** quality/operating outcome; **I** proposed implementation; **C** business/solution constraint; **V** vague. Some statements span categories; the primary reading is provided in the key.

**Kubernetes** is a platform for operating packaged software workloads; **Redis** is an in-memory information store often used for caches; **WebSockets** provide a continuing two-way client/server connection. These are examples of possible mechanisms, not evidence of qualities. A **jurisdiction** is the territory or authority whose rules apply; exact obligations need confirmation.

## 92. Scenario exercises: ten systems

For each, identify top qualities, possible flexibility, trade-offs, and measurements before reading. Each solution assumes the stated context and preserves security/applicable obligations.

**Tick**, in a game context, is one scheduled state-update step, like a repeated beat; **divergence** means views disagree under the model. These matter for timing and state correctness, not just page-loading speed.

| System/context | Worked priorities | Flexibility/trade-off | Measurements |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banking transfers | Correctness, durability, authority, consistency, auditability | Delay or pending state may be preferable to incorrect rapid completion | Wrong/duplicate outcomes, retained records, access matrix, RTO/RPO |
| Private chat | Delivery reliability, history survival, privacy, latency | Offline delivery has different timing; diagnostic content restricted | Connected delivery percentiles, acceptance-to-retrieval, duplicate counts |
| Public social feed | Useful availability, latency, scale, visibility safety | Informational counts may be stale; quality versus transfer cost | Journey latency, good-request fraction, freshness, peak capacity |
| Real-time game | Interaction delay, responsiveness, state integrity | Geographic distribution versus cost/coordination | Tick/action timing, divergence checks, cheating-risk controls, concurrent sessions |
| Healthcare appointment/records | Access, privacy, reliable records, recovery | Appointment booking and urgent care contexts differ | Role/relationship tests, record survival, audit completeness, recovery drills |
| Video viewing | Playback start/stalls, throughput, scale, cost | Adaptive media quality and distributed copies versus cost | Startup distribution, stall fraction, bytes/useful minute, unit cost |
| IoT meters | Reliable ingestion, device identity, capacity, recovery | Delay may be tolerable for reporting; safety-related controls differ | Accepted/retained readings, duplicates, freshness, device connection recovery |
| Online store sale | Checkout correctness, protected orders, capacity, access | Optional recommendations can degrade; spare capacity costs money | Inventory/charge tests, p95, good checkout fraction, cost at peak |
| Weekly analytics | Correct reports, access, recovery, cost | Longer processing/freshness delay often acceptable | Completion by deadline, reconciled totals, query costs |
| Internal permissions tool | Authorization, auditability, safe changes, operability | Small scale can avoid expensive geographic redundancy | Action matrix, audit completeness, rollback/diagnosis, actual usage |

For every scenario, ask what happens when measurements are missed and what evidence supports the target. A reasonable priority without measurable scope is still incomplete.

## 94. Common interview follow-ups

| Follow-up | Strong response pattern |
|---|---|
| Why 99.99%? | Link to user/business consequence and agreed measurement window; if merely assumed, say so and compare a lower target’s impact/cost |
| Can reads be stale? | Identify operation/data and consequence; define allowed freshness and read-after-write behavior rather than a system-wide slogan |
| What happens during region loss? | State which useful functions/acknowledged information must survive, surviving capacity, RTO/RPO, routing, and tested evidence |
| What if traffic grows 10×? | Recalculate work/storage, find shared bottlenecks, preserve quality limits, compare incremental scaling costs |
| What if infrastructure cost is too high? | Identify costly work/copies/transfers, remove unjustified overhead, consider bounded deferral/staleness, preserve critical obligations |
| Why add a cache? | Name the measured repeated-read problem, freshness/security rules, hit/miss behavior, and full cost |
| Why not stronger consistency everywhere? | Explain critical guarantees and unnecessary coordination costs for relaxed informational operations |
| How know the design works? | Cite representative benchmarks, outcome checks, fault/recovery drills, operational evidence, and unresolved assumptions |
| What if a dependency is down? | Define time limits, safe pending/retry behavior, degraded functionality, and business correction |
| What if targets conflict? | State consequence/priority, propose measurable scope or alternative designs, and seek agreement on unresolved policy |

The general answer pattern is **requirement → assumption/evidence → choice → trade-off → verification**. It keeps the discussion about solving the actual problem.

## 95. Flashcards: 52 cards

**Q1:** What is an NFR?  
**A:** A required quality or operating condition of a system or its behavior.

**Q2:** What is an FR?  
**A:** A required observable behavior: what the system must do.

**Q3:** What is a software system?  
**A:** Connected software and resources providing a purpose through inputs, work, and outputs.

**Q4:** What is a client?  
**A:** A party requesting work, such as a phone application.

**Q5:** What is a server?  
**A:** Software accepting requests; the word also refers to the computer running it.

**Q6:** What is a request/response?  
**A:** A request asks for work; a response communicates an outcome or information.

**Q7:** What is scalability?  
**A:** Accommodating growth while preserving defined qualities within a viable resource strategy.

**Q8:** Scale up versus out?  
**A:** Make a machine more capable versus distribute work across more machines.

**Q9:** What is a bottleneck?  
**A:** A limiting resource or step restricting the system’s useful work.

**Q10:** What is availability?  
**A:** The ability to provide acceptable scoped service when needed.

**Q11:** Time availability formula?  
**A:** Uptime divided by total measured time, multiplied by 100 for percentage.

**Q12:** Request availability formula?  
**A:** Good eligible requests divided by all eligible requests under the chosen criteria/window.

**Q13:** What is reliability?  
**A:** Correct specified performance over the relevant conditions and time.

**Q14:** Availability versus reliability?  
**A:** Usable service now versus correct performance across conditions/time; measurements can overlap.

**Q15:** What is durability?  
**A:** Acknowledged stored information survives the stated failures for the stated retention.

**Q16:** Durability versus backup?  
**A:** An outcome guarantee versus one possible recovery-copy technique.

**Q17:** Backup versus replica?  
**A:** A retained recoverable point-in-time copy versus a maintained copy often used for serving/survival.

**Q18:** What is consistency?  
**A:** Rules about allowed observations and update ordering across operations and copies.

**Q19:** What does linearizability require?  
**A:** Operations appear in a single order respecting real-time completion-before-start relationships.

**Q20:** What does eventual consistency promise?  
**A:** Convergence after updates stop and propagation/repair proceeds; not a universal time bound.

**Q21:** What is stale data?  
**A:** A view reflecting an older update rather than the required latest observation.

**Q22:** What is latency?  
**A:** Elapsed time between specified operation start and finish boundaries.

**Q23:** What is throughput?  
**A:** Completed useful work per unit time.

**Q24:** Latency versus throughput?  
**A:** One operation’s time versus completed amount per period.

**Q25:** What is p95?  
**A:** A threshold covering 95% of measured observations under the stated computation method.

**Q26:** Why not average alone?  
**A:** It can conceal tail delays, failures, and uneven user experiences.

**Q27:** What is responsiveness?  
**A:** Prompt, useful interface reaction and feedback, distinct from final completion time.

**Q28:** What is fault tolerance?  
**A:** Maintaining specified useful service despite named component faults.

**Q29:** What is resilience?  
**A:** Withstanding, adapting to, and recovering from disruption while providing useful service.

**Q30:** What is recoverability?  
**A:** Restoring acceptable operation and information after disruption.

**Q31:** What is RTO?  
**A:** Target maximum elapsed time to restore the defined acceptable service after the chosen starting event.

**Q32:** What is RPO?  
**A:** Target maximum acceptable update-loss time span for defined information/failures.

**Q33:** What does a timeout prove?  
**A:** A timely response was not received; outside work may still have succeeded.

**Q34:** Why safe retry behavior?  
**A:** Repeated attempts must not cause unintended duplicate business effects or overload recovery.

**Q35:** What is security?  
**A:** Protection against unauthorized actions and relevant threats to systems/information.

**Q36:** What is privacy?  
**A:** Appropriate collection, use, sharing, and retention of personal information.

**Q37:** Authentication versus authorization?  
**A:** Identity checking versus checking permission for an action.

**Q38:** What is least privilege?  
**A:** Grant only the access needed for a responsibility.

**Q39:** What is maintainability?  
**A:** How effectively a system can be understood, diagnosed, modified, and verified.

**Q40:** What is operability?  
**A:** How safely and effectively people can run the system for real users.

**Q41:** What is extensibility?  
**A:** Adding a relevant capability with bounded disruption and effort.

**Q42:** What is modularity?  
**A:** Clear responsibility boundaries among parts, including within one application.

**Q43:** Cohesion versus coupling?  
**A:** How well responsibilities belong together versus how much parts depend on one another’s details.

**Q44:** Portability versus interoperability?  
**A:** Moving among environments versus correctly exchanging/using information with other systems.

**Q45:** What is testability?  
**A:** How readily expected behavior and qualities can be verified reproducibly.

**Q46:** Observability versus monitoring?  
**A:** Investigating behavior from outputs versus checking selected known measurements/conditions.

**Q47:** Auditability versus ordinary logging?  
**A:** Accountable reconstruction under required protection/retention versus event recording generally.

**Q48:** What is accessibility?  
**A:** Intended functions usable by people with differing abilities.

**Q49:** Compliance versus security?  
**A:** Applicable obligation/evidence fulfillment versus protection against relevant threats.

**Q50:** Cost versus energy efficiency?  
**A:** Useful outcomes per justified spending versus useful outcomes per energy/resources; both preserve required quality.

**Q51:** SLI versus SLO versus SLA?  
**A:** Chosen measurement, target over a window, and customer agreement with obligations/consequences.

**Q52:** What makes an NFR testable?  
**A:** Defined quality, target, scope, conditions, measurement, rationale, and verification evidence.

#### Short-answer sample solutions

1. A restaurant’s connected staff/tools turn an order into a meal or explanation; software/resources similarly turn requests into results.
2. FR: users submit supported photos and receive acceptance/rejection. NFR: 95% become viewable within two seconds under named file/network/load conditions. Separate behavior and timing.
3. Availability is useful access when needed; reliability is correct performance over conditions/time. Metrics can overlap if good requests include correctness.
4. Durability is retained acknowledged information surviving specified faults; recoverability is restoring acceptable operation/information. Safely retained files may take too long to restore.
5. Latency is measured elapsed time for an operation; responsiveness is prompt useful interface reaction. Immediate honest progress can coexist with longer completion.
6. p95 covers 95% of observations at its threshold under the specified method. Scope failures/outstanding attempts, measurement window, and interpolation/nearest-rank choice.
7. RTO ≤ one hour means verified restoration within that interval from the defined incident start. RPO ≤ five minutes allows no more than five minutes’ relevant update-loss span in that failure model.
8. Monitoring watches chosen conditions; observability supports investigating behavior, including unexpected situations, from useful outputs. Tools overlap but outcomes differ.
9. Portability concerns moving to named environments; interoperability concerns correct exchanges with other systems. Both require semantics and verified compatibility.
10. ID, attribute, description, target, metric, scope, conditions, method, priority, rationale, owner/source, open decisions. Rationale justifies the target and guides trade-offs/change.

#### Scenario sample solutions

1. Estimate representative mix, burst duration, information size, arrival/completion rates, queues, and resource/dependency limits. Require preserved latency/error/correctness at 5,000/second within budget; identify bottleneck before choosing expansion.
2. The success acknowledgement’s durability/reliability promise failed if it covered restart. Compare retained accepted operations against reliable financial evidence; preserve/reconcile pending differences and fix acknowledgement protection.
3. Informational likes may tolerate agreed staleness. Permission staleness may expose private content; establish access guarantees separately. One consistency slogan cannot decide both.
4. The recovery point could meet five-minute RPO if usable and complete; two-hour restoration misses any tighter RTO, such as 30 minutes. Include verification and dependencies.
5. Define graceful degradation: optional recommendation failure must not block supported browse/checkout. Isolate dependency/failure paths and verify a fault drill.
6. Diagnostic value competes with privacy/security, retention, and cost. Require sufficient status/correlation evidence without unnecessary content; preserve justified audit information under its own rules.
7. Core sign-in/recovery is keyboard-operable with meaningful labels, visible focus, accessible error/status feedback under the adopted evaluation scope. Verify manually plus appropriate automated checks.
8. Reliable individual actor identity, authorization context, order/amount/currency, time, reason, request identity, and protected outcome evidence. A shared credential cannot by itself establish who acted.
9. Ask which geography/failure/recovery outcome it serves; evaluate measured latency, independence, operating effort, and lifecycle cost. Remove or redesign unjustified complexity while preserving actual obligations.
10. No, if it violates the authoritative confirmed-price rule. Cache suitable browsing information, revalidate/reconfirm agreed checkout amounts, and assess all requirements rather than p95 alone.
